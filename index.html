02 Abstract

Geometry is the interface — and the vulnerability.

3D manipulation policies derive actions from explicit scene geometry rather than color images alone, enabling them to reason over metric distances, object relations, and robot-scene reachability. This geometric interface is also a vulnerability: commodity RGB-D sensors return missing measurements, boundary artifacts, and plausible but wrong surfaces around transparent, reflective, thin, or textureless objects.

We study this failure under realistic depth corruption and observe a sharp depth-noise cliff across modern 3D policies, even when they are trained with matched noisy depth. This residual gap motivates a spatial-encoding approach: corrupted depth becomes entangled with the token coordinates and geometry features consumed by the action expert. We propose Noise-Invariant Spatial Encoding (NISE), a 3D policy architecture that learns trustworthy geometry features even facing imperfect depth. NISE uses Geometric Cross-View Self-Supervision to shape shared policy features with calibrated multi-view depth, and Gaussian Marginalized 3D Attention to carry position uncertainty into deployed attention rather than treating every lifted token as exact.

Across simulated benchmarks with sensor-realistic corruption and real-robot tasks involving transparent and reflective materials, NISE improves depth-noise robustness while preserving clean-depth performance, supporting 3D policies designed for commodity RGB-D deployment rather than clean-depth benchmarks alone.

03 The depth-corruption cliff

When clean-depth assumptions meet real sensors.

Modern 3D manipulation policies convert RGB-D into point clouds or depth-lifted tokens, yet they are developed and evaluated on clean simulator depth. Commercial RGB-D sensors instead return structured corruption: missing measurements, boundary artifacts, and plausible-but-wrong geometry, worst around transparent, reflective, thin, and textureless objects.

Under realistic depth corruption, current 3D policies fall off a sharp cliff, the fragility generalizes across visual backbones and action-generation mechanisms, and training on matched noisy depth recovers only part of the lost performance. NISE addresses this at the architecture level.

We model the corruption families that commodity depth sensors actually exhibit — missing measurements (holes), boundary artifacts (edge bleeding), and plausible-but-wrong surfaces — and evaluate at calibrated clean, mild, and saturated levels. The presets bracket sensor statistics measured with RealSense D405 and Azure Kinect; saturated is the stress-test endpoint.

How we model it

A controlled diagnostic, not a simulator.

On benchmark depth, we inject these same structural failure modes at calibrated severities, then measure how policies hold up. Sweep the severity, or peel the corruption apart family by family — both are produced by the exact pipeline used in the experiments, not a web re-creation.

A controlled diagnostic, not a simulator. We inject the structural failure modes real depth sensors are known to produce — missing returns, boundary bleed, plausible-but-wrong surfaces — at calibrated severities to probe policy robustness. We make no claim that it replicates any specific sensor. We compare NISE with six baseline architectures (3DDA, RVT-2, SAM2Act, DP3, FlowPolicy, and ManiFlow) across two benchmarks (RLBench and MetaWorld), and test transfer to real robots under naturally occurring depth corruption.

04 Method — NISE

Features that stay stable when the depth is wrong.

The thinking

What a 3D policy needs is geometry features that stay stable when the underlying depth is imperfect. That implies two moves: shape the shared features toward view-consistent geometry during training (GCVSS), and let the deployed attention carry how much to trust each lifted token’s position (GM3A).

GCVSS Training-time

Geometric Cross-View Self-Supervision

The cross-view geometry head learns from calibrated multi-view depth to encourage view-consistent geometry in the shared policy features. It needs no clean-depth ground truth and is removed at deployment.

GM3A Retained at inference

Gaussian Marginalized 3D Attention

A per-token residual predictor adjusts positional attenuation around an analytic Gaussian sensor prior. Regularization anchors these learned adjustments to the prior, so uncertain coordinates influence attention according to their spatial reliability.

GCVSS · the supervision signal

Two noisy views, one geometry.

GCVSS needs no clean-depth ground truth. Both cameras are noisy and neither one alone pins the geometry; their cross-view agreement is the signal. Watch the second view lock it in.

Both views are noisy, and there is no clean depth to copy from. One camera alone sees a thin, one-sided shell and cannot pin the geometry; the second viewpoint closes the missing faces and locks it. Because the two cameras carry independent noise on the same surfaces, their cross-view agreement is a learnable target — GCVSS supervises it Noise2Noise-style, with no clean-depth ground truth required. Right: warping Kinect into the D405 frame, the warped depth lands on the actual depth across those shared surfaces.

GM3A · the dampening field

Trust geometry by its reliability.

Conceptual illustration of the mechanism

Depth error grows with range — and worsens on transparent, reflective, and boundary geometry, exactly where a gripper makes contact. GM3A derives a Gaussian attenuation prior for uncertain token positions (αprior = exp(−σ²ω²/2)): reliable coordinates keep their high-frequency geometric phase, uncertain ones are dampened. A learned per-token residual adapts these coefficients. Watch the procedure unfold on the real grasp scene below.

Reliability, not certainty. The procedure is a conceptual illustration of GM3A. The sensor prior — each token’s position uncertainty under the camera’s range law — is real; the per-token adaptation on transparent and boundary geometry illustrates what GM3A is designed to learn as a residual on top of that prior. GM3A dampens brittle high-frequency phase where geometry is uncertain — it does not reconstruct hidden surfaces — so corrupted depth steers the action less and the policy leans on trustworthy structure.

Ablation · mild noise Under noisy-depth training and mild-noise evaluation, the frozen-ViT+DPT control reaches 75.5%; adding GCVSS and GM3A together raises it to 85.7%. GCVSS provides the main representation gain; GM3A adds complementary gains once the features are shaped.
